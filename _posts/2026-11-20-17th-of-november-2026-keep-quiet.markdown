# 17th of November 2026 - keep quiet

November the 17th this year has come and passed. Another opportunity for the Greek school to flag the most important event in the Greek history of the last ~50 years has been missed. For those who are not aware what Greeks commemorate on the 17th of November each year, there is lots of reading material online, e.g. [Athens Polytechnic Uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athens_Polytechnic_uprising), and for those who prefer reading in Greek, there is a good description of events here: [Selidodiktis](https://selidodeiktis.edu.gr/2026/11/13/%ce%b1%cf%80%cf%8c-%cf%84%ce%b1-%ce%b3%ce%b5%ce%b3%ce%bf%ce%bd%cf%8c%cf%84%ce%b1-%cf%84%ce%b7%cf%82-%ce%bd%ce%bf%ce%bc%ce%b9%ce%ba%ce%ae%cf%82-%cf%83%cf%84%ce%b7%ce%bd-%ce%b5%ce%be%ce%ad%ce%b3%ce%b5/).

What happened on the 17th of November 1973 was the most important event in our recent history. A popular uprising that began at university departments triggered the fall of the dictatorship and the restoration of democracy in Greece. People lost their lives to remove the dictators. The mobilisation was too massive for the police to intervene, hence the orders to bring in the army to attack and kill protesters.

This event is commemorated nationally in Greece and primarily in schools. School assemblies are held where students deliver carefully prepared material. There are no lessons on the day. Some students travel to the site of the uprising to deposit flowers. The event is as significant as any other national celebration in the country.

I have been involved with the Greek School in Cardiff for over 12 years, and my question has always been:

**Why doesn't the Greek School in Cardiff commemorate this significant event?**

I have thought hard over the years to understand what the reasons may be. In the past, other Greek schools in Britain have commemorated the 17th of November and this has been reported in KEA's newsletter, so there should not be any restrictions from KEA.

The event is a formal event in the Greek government's official school calendar, so I do not see the Greek government preventing it from happening either.

There is lots of material available for teachers to prepare an assembly or even classes related to the 17th of November, so I do not think it is a difficult event to prepare in terms of time and effort for educators.

Why, I mean, really, why don't we want to teach our kids that a dictatorship is a bad thing for people, and that our parents and grand-parents fought to get rid of it? Why don't we want to teach our kids the value of democracy and the importance of social struggle?

As parents, are you not concerned that this important chapter in Greek history is ignored every year?

If you do, then get active:

* Talk to other Greek School parents.
* Ask the teachers of the Greek School to plan a commemoration of the 17th of November for 2027.
* Bring the issue up at the next Greek School AGM.

This is our School and our kids are here to learn not only the language but also the critical historical events that shaped our country.
